# E8.4 — Transcript compression

A committed session's cold half becomes a summary. Two pieces:

- `session.Digest` and `Store.Compress` — the summary of everything said through
  a given sequence, written as `DIGEST` beside the log, plus `Transcript.Hot()`,
  the turns the digest does not reach. The reduced transcript is those two read
  together.
- `commit.Compression` and the `reduce` stage — the cold half rendered as a
  document, summarised through the gateway, recorded on the session.

The ticket's acceptance is one test in each layer, plus one over the real
endpoint:

```
$ go test ./internal/core/commit/ ./internal/core/session/ \
```

## What the pattern was

Compression writes a derived file beside the log and never into it. `Compress`
takes a summary and the sequence it covers through; the store validates that the
sequence is one somebody actually said and is not behind the digest already
standing, and nothing else about the summary is its business. The model call is
`commit`'s, so `session` stays a store that knows no provider — the same split as
`memory` and extraction.

The summariser is reached through the gateway accessor E7.1 already built, so
compression needed no new port. `Summarise` takes a document and a sentence
budget, so the seam is a renderer: `conversationOf` turns the cold messages into
one, exactly as `turnsOf` maps a transcript into the extractor's vocabulary.

## What surprised me

**"Compression" and "reduction" are not the same verb, and the roadmap splits
them across two epics.** E8.4 makes the summary; E8.10 moves the cold half
aside. Reading E8.4 as "drop the old messages" would have put a destructive verb
on an append-only store six tickets before the ticket that wants one. What makes
the reduction real without deleting anything is `Hot()`: a caller reads the
digest plus the turns past it and never touches what the summary stands for.

**Which of the two writes goes first is decided by which one is idempotent.**
There is no transaction across the session store and the memory store, so one of
them is second and can be lost. A digest written twice converges — the second
commit finds the same coverage and calls no endpoint — while a memory written
twice is exactly the duplicate E8.5 exists to prevent. So both endpoints answer
first, then the digest, then the memories, and the interleaving that survives an
interruption is the harmless one.

**Re-summarising the whole cold half beats folding the new part into the
standing digest.** Folding is cheaper and it is a summary of a summary: it
drifts from the conversation on every commit and there is no way back to the
turns it came from. Re-reading costs one model call over text that is already
there.

**A rendering choice needs the real summariser to be testable.** The first
version of the end-to-end case joined turns with a blank line and passed just as
well when mutated to a single newline, because every turn in it ended in a full
stop and `termsum` cuts at those either way. Making one cold turn end without
punctuation is what turned the test into evidence: joined by one newline,
`termsum` answers `"user: always use tabs assistant: Noted."` — a sentence
neither turn said. A stand-in that ignores its input could never have failed
that.

## What I would do differently

`Compression` is passed to `commit.New` rather than read from config, so `Keep`
and `Sentences` are still a caller's literal. That is Tenet 6 half-satisfied: the
numbers are not hardcoded in the stage, but nothing resolves them from a file or
an environment yet. E8.2 is where a transport first constructs a committer, and
that is the honest moment to add the config block rather than inventing one for a
constructor with one caller.

## What I chose not to build

No archival. The messages a digest covers are all still there, and moving them
aside is E8.10's whole ticket.

No route and no wiring in `main`, for the reason E8.3 gave: E8.2 is where a
transport first sees any of this.

No standalone `Digest` read verb. `Get` carries it, and `commit` needs the
transcript in the same call anyway; a second verb would be a second answer to
what a session is.

No compression on read. A session is compressed when it is committed and at no
other time, so nothing has to reason about a `Get` that costs a model call.

## What I still do not understand

Whether `Keep` should count messages or something closer to what a context
window actually spends. Counting messages is honest about what the store knows —
it has sequences, not tokens — and it is wrong in the way that matters: two short
turns and one long one are the same `Keep` and not the same budget. E8.2 builds
the prompt and is the first thing that will feel the difference.

Whether a digest should say which summariser made it. Two commits under
different chains produce digests that are not the same kind of object — one
extractive, one abstractive — and nothing recorded says which is being read. It
is the same question `Source` raised for memories, and the answer probably wants
to be the same shape for both.

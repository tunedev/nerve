# E3.6 — Completion notification

A caller blocks on a job finishing. It publishes nothing new and listens on
nothing new: a completion goes to the exchange the relay already publishes to,
under a key that says which job and what became of it.

```
caller   Expect("index/42")                        → a Future
caller   Write /queue/jobs/index/42                → journal, then relay → exchange
worker   Listener says "index/42"                  → Read, handle
worker   Publish "done/ok/index/42"                → exchange
caller   Listener says "done/ok/index/42"          → the Future settles
```

Three things landed, all in `internal/adapters/outbound/queuefs`:

| Piece | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Reporting` | A `Handler` wrapped so it says what became of the job when it returns. |
| `Waiter` | The awaiting end: holds what callers expect, settled by a `Listener`. |
| `Future` | One completion before it has arrived. `Wait(ctx)` blocks; the result is readable after. |

## What the pattern was

**A completion is addressed, not carried.** Everything a waiter needs is in the
key — `done/<outcome>/<job>` — so `Notify`'s key-only signature, which exists so
a work notification cannot become a way to distribute a body, turns out to be
exactly enough. The waiter never comes back to the tree.

**Waiting is broadcast; working is competing.** A worker claims because only one
of them may do a job. A waiter only hears, because every caller waiting on a job
needs the answer, and a claim would let one of them take another's. That single
difference decides everything else: a waiter's listener needs a queue of its own
on the broker, and the offline suite cannot prove it — two `memListener`s always
both hear, so the case that matters is the one against RabbitMQ, where sharing a
queue name makes the second caller wait out its deadline.

**Registration before publication, so there is no race to lose.** `Expect`
returns the future before the job is written. A completion for a key nobody
expected has nobody to tell, and a caller that published first could be that
nobody — the job may be done before its producer's next statement runs. The
alternative was remembering every finished key against a caller that might turn
up, which is a map nothing bounds.

**The one thing on this bus that is not recorded first.** A job is durable state
and is journaled before anyone is told it exists. A completion is a fact about a
job that is no longer queued and that nothing ever reads back — recording one
puts a row in Postgres that no claim removes. So losing the broker costs a
waiter its notification and leaves it to its deadline, and costs no work: the
same bargain the bus already makes one level down.

## What surprised me

**The claim, which is what makes the work bus correct, is what makes it wrong
for completions.** The first design was symmetric and pleasing — a waiter is a
worker on the `done` prefix, awaiting is just being a worker — and it breaks the
moment two processes wait at once. Both are told about both completions; the
first to claim takes the body, and the other's future never settles. The
descriptor said so all along: `ReadDestructive` and `Broadcast` are different
things, and a completion is the second.

**Publishing straight to `Broker` felt like the violation and is the opposite of
one.** Every other message on this bus is published by writing a path, and
reaching for the port instead reads like a shortcut past the mount. But the
journal is there to make losing the broker lose nothing, and a completion has
nothing to lose. Writing it through the tree would have bought durability for a
fact nobody can read and left a table that grows forever.

**A failed job needed one bit and got it for free.** Without it a caller waiting
on a job whose handler refused waits out its deadline, which cannot tell failed
from slow. The outcome fits in the element after `done/`, so `ErrRefused` costs
a key element and no port change. Why it failed does not travel — that is where
the worker ran.

## What I chose not to build

**A durable record of what became of a job.** Nothing can ask "did job X
finish?" after the fact; a caller that was not waiting when the completion went
past has no way to find out. That is a real gap and the honest place to close it
is wherever job results are eventually kept, not in a notification.

**Retry, redelivery or a dead letter for completions.** A completion the broker
refuses is reported to the worker and joined onto whatever the handler said. The
run ends either way.

**Wiring into `cmd/nerved`.** Same reason E3.4 gave: there is no handler to run
yet, and inventing config for which queue a waiter binds to before anything
awaits anything is config with no caller.

## What I still do not understand

Whether `Expect` should have been `Await(ctx, key)` with the registration
implicit. It reads better at one call site and it makes the race invisible
rather than absent, which is how a caller ends up publishing first and waiting
forever. Two calls that must be in order is the honest shape, but it is a shape
a caller can still get wrong, and nothing here catches it.

Whether a waiter should hold a queue that outlives it. A durable queue per
waiting process is right while a process waits; a process that dies leaves a
queue on the broker collecting completions nobody will read. Autodelete would
fix that and would also throw away the binding a restart wants back.

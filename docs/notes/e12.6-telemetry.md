# E12.6 — OpenTelemetry traces, metrics and logs

`vfs.Recorder` is a core port with one method: a call began, and here is what
it returned. `vfs.WithTelemetry` is the fourth decorator over the port and puts
every verb through it. `internal/adapters/outbound/telemetry` is the
OpenTelemetry side — one report becomes a span, a measurement in
`nerve.mount.call.duration`, and a log line, all three carrying the same trace.
`Registry.Build` puts the wrapper on every mount, outermost.
`telemetry-endpoint` is a setting, empty by default, and `make observe` starts
something to point it at.

## What the pattern was

The fourth decorator, and the first that reports outward instead of deciding
something. The timeout, the retry and the breaker all change what the call does;
this one changes nothing and only says what happened, which is why the port has
one method and the wrapper has no state.

The port keeps the SDK out of the core, and the calibration rule is satisfied
without arguing: the second implementation is `NopRecorder`, and it is not a
stub — it is what a build with no collector actually runs. That is also why the
registry holds a recorder from the moment it is constructed rather than a nil
one it checks. Wiring that only exists in production is wiring nobody tests.

Ordering is where the design decision is. Under the retry, the recorder times
one attempt; a call that took three seconds across three attempts then reads as
three fast calls with the waiting nowhere, which is the exact question the
ticket exists to answer. Outermost it times what the caller waited for. The
attempt-level breakdown is the thing deliberately not built: it needs the retry
wrapper to report too, and that is a second increment with its own unknown.

The recorder is also the only layer that knows which mount it is on. Everything
below it holds a plugin; the spec — prefix and kind — exists in `Build` and
nowhere deeper, which is what makes `Build` the only place the wrapper can go.

## What surprised me

**`WithEndpointURL` swallows a URL it cannot parse.** A first test asserted that
building a pipeline with `://nope` fails at boot. It does not: all three
exporters log the parse error through the OTel internal logger, keep their own
default endpoint, and return a working exporter. A process told to export to a
typo would have exported to localhost instead, quietly.

```
2026/08/28 03:10:09 internal_logging.go:50: "msg"="otlptrace: parse endpoint url"
  "error"="parse \"://nope\": missing protocol scheme" "url"="://nope"
--- FAIL: TestAnUnreadableEndpointFailsAtBoot
    New accepted an endpoint that is not a URL
```

The fix belongs where every other setting is already checked, not in a second
guard beside the exporter: `Validate` rejects a `telemetry-endpoint` that is not
an `http`/`https` URL, and the failing test moved to `internal/config`. That
needed one new validator — `optional` — because this is the first setting with a
real "off". Every other setting has a default that works; this one has empty
meaning "export nothing", and empty is not a boot failure.

**The path is a span attribute and never a metric label.** A histogram labelled
by path is one series per file, which is a cardinality bomb rather than a
metric. Splitting it — path into the trace, outcome into the metric — is what
makes both usable, and the outcome label is free: `Classify` already sorted the
error for the retry, so the metric says whether another attempt would have
helped without deciding anything itself.

**Two lists of log levels had quietly become possible.** `Validate` accepted
four names from a slice, and the new `LogLevelValue` resolved them through a
map. Nothing would have failed until someone added a fifth to one of them and
the process booted logging at the wrong threshold. The slice is now derived from
the map, sorted by level, so the names accepted and the levels resolved cannot
disagree.

**Logs need two destinations, not one.** Sending them only to the collector
loses them exactly when they are wanted — a collector that is down is the
incident. `fanout` is thirty lines of `slog.Handler` over two handlers, and its
one subtlety is that `Enabled` has to be the union: a stderr handler at `info`
must not decide for a bridge at `debug`.

## What I would do differently

Name the port `Recorder` from the start. The first draft called it `Tracer`,
which is one of the three signals and a type name in `go.opentelemetry.io/otel`
besides — the core would have been describing the adapter's vocabulary back to
it.

## What I still do not understand

Whether the transfer half of a stream should be measured. The open is a call and
is recorded; the transfer is a separate lifetime, which is the split
`WithTimeout` makes too — it bounds the open by a deadline and the transfer by
idleness, because a healthy download is as long as the entry. A stream that
spends forty seconds moving bytes steadily is not slow; one that spends forty
seconds nearly idle is, and neither is visible today. Recording it means a
second report ending at `Close`, which is one call becoming two spans — and
whether that is one call with a long tail or two calls is a question the trace
model has an answer to and I do not yet know it.

Whether the logs should carry the mount call at all once there are inbound
transports. Today a report is the only thing nerved logs, so the line is the
whole log; with an HTTP or gRPC adapter above it the same facts would be in the
span and the line would be duplication. The level rule — transient at `warn`,
everything else at `debug` — is what keeps that tolerable for now.

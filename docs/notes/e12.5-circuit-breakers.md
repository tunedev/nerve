# E12.5 — Circuit breakers with domain fallbacks

`vfs.WithBreaker` wraps a plugin so that enough transient failures in a row stop
it being called: the circuit opens, every verb answers `vfs.ErrUnavailable`
without reaching the far end, and after the cooldown one call goes through to
find out whether it is back. `Registry.Build` puts one breaker on every mount,
between the bound and the retry. `breaker-threshold` and `breaker-cooldown` are
settings.

## What the pattern was

The third decorator over the same port, and the first that remembers anything.
The timeout and the retry are pure: they hold a value, apply it, and forget the
call. A breaker is state shared by every caller of a mount, so it is a pointer
behind a mutex, and everything else about the wrapper — forward, re-wrap, keep a
refusal a refusal — is the shape the other two already have.

Classification is what makes it a breaker rather than a failure counter. Only a
`Retryable` error moves the count, so the judgement `Classify` already makes for
the retry is the one the breaker inherits: `fs.ErrNotExist` a hundred times is a
mount that is working, and counting it would open a circuit on a caller's typo.

## What surprised me

**The open question from E12.3 had a sharper answer than either option.** That
note framed it as "outside the retry it counts sequences, inside it counts
attempts", and left the choice open. Inside is right, and the reason is
arithmetic rather than taste: outside, the circuit needs `attempts × threshold`
calls to open, and every one of them lands on a dependency that is already
failing — the breaker takes longest to trip exactly when it is needed most.
Inside, the retry's own attempts trip it, and the attempts still to come are
refused at once, so the sequence that opened the circuit is also the last one to
reach the far end.

**The classification the retry needed from the breaker was already correct by
default.** `FailFast` is the zero value of `Class`, so `ErrUnavailable` — which
`Classify` has never been told anything about — is fail-fast without a line of
code. That is the same default that makes an unclassified plugin error
fail-fast, doing a second job nobody designed it for. It is asserted in a test
rather than left implicit, because the whole composition rests on it: any other
answer and a retry that met an open circuit would spend its remaining attempts
learning what the breaker had just decided.

**"Domain fallback" turned out to mean the answers that need no far end.** There
is no second provider to fail over to — nothing in this repo has an ordered
provider chain yet — so the fallback is what a mount can still say when it
cannot be called. `Capabilities` is the whole of it, and it is enough to be
worth naming: it is not I/O, so a caller can still learn that a path is
read-destructive or append-only while it cannot learn what the path holds. The
other half is structural rather than a fallback at all: each mount owns its
circuit, so a broken subtree refuses in microseconds and the rest of the tree
never learns of it.

**One probe needs no half-open flag.** Letting the probe through by moving the
cooldown forward — `until = now + cooldown` as the probe leaves — refuses
everything that arrives behind it for free, and a failed probe needs no handling
at all because the wait it restarted is already correct. The state is two
fields, and the burst test that would have caught a missing flag passes without
one.

## What I chose not to build

Per-mount policies, for the same reason the timeout and the retry do not have
them: the override belongs in `MountSpec` and lands with the first remote plugin
that needs a different one. It is now the third setting waiting on that.

A half-open window of more than one call. Real breakers ramp traffic back up;
this one resumes it in full when a probe answers. With one process and no
traffic to shed, a ramp is a curve with nothing to measure it against.

Counters of what the breaker did. How many circuits are open, and how often, is
the first thing an operator will ask, and it is E12.6 and E12.7 rather than a
field on this struct.

An off switch. `breaker-threshold` must be at least 1, so there is no spelling
that turns the breaker off in config — the zero value does, but only in code, in
tests and in the composition of a wrapper that was never given a policy.

## What I still do not understand

Whether a caller should be able to tell an open circuit from a dependency that
is down. Right now it cannot: `ErrUnavailable` says "this mount is not being
called", not "this mount is broken", and the difference matters to anything
deciding whether to try a different subtree. Wrapping the failure that opened
the circuit would say both, at the cost of an error whose `errors.Is` answers
change with time. Nothing needs it yet.

What the threshold should be for a mount that is slow rather than dead. A far
end answering every call in just over the timeout produces an unbroken streak of
`DeadlineExceeded`, so the circuit opens on the fifth — which is right if it is
overloaded and wrong if the timeout is simply too tight for it. The breaker
cannot tell those apart and neither can the config; a per-mount timeout would
make the question go away for the mount that raised it.
